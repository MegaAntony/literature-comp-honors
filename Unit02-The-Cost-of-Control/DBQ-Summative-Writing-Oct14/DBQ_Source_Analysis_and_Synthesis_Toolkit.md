# 9th Grade Honors English: DBQ Essay Prep & Study Guide
**Essential Question:** Is the censorship of books and information dangerous to society? Why or why not?  
**Test Date:** Wednesday, Oct 14 (In-Class DBQ)  
**Teacher:** Ms. Grote | Literature & Composition I Honors (Room 1314)

---

## What Ms. Grote Actually Wants to See on Wednesday

Don't write like a machine or dump a list of fancy vocabulary words. Honors teachers don't grade on big words; they grade on **clear thinking, strong evidence, and original voice**. 

To get an A on this in-class essay:
1. **Answer the question directly:** Don't beat around the bush. State clearly why cutting people off from real ideas destroys human empathy and leads to disaster.
2. **Blend your texts together:** Don't write one paragraph that is only about Billy Collins and another paragraph that is only about Bradbury. Put them in conversation with each other in every body paragraph.
3. **Unpack the quotes:** Never leave a quote hanging. Explain the *details* of what the author is saying and why it proves your point.

## The 4 Packet Sources & How to Use Them

---

### Source 1: "Rain" by Billy Collins (Poem)
* **What page of packet:** Page 1
* **The Quote to use:**
  > *"But there were no more books,*  
  > *just the curling up of the people*  
  > *quietly in corners and doorways."* (Collins lines 25–28)
* **What it really means (Inference):**
  * When people read, they usually "curl up" on a couch or in bed with a book because it feels safe, cozy, and relaxing. 
  * But here, Collins flips that completely upside down. The books are gone, but people are still curling up. Why? Because they are terrified, helpless, and frozen. They are not curling up out of happiness; they are curling up like wounded animals hiding in dark corners. 
  * And notice the word *"quietly."* They don't scream or fight back. Without books, they don't even have the words left to explain why they are hurting.
* **Why this proves censorship is dangerous (Analysis):**
  * Censorship doesn't just destroy paper; it destroys the human imagination and our ability to feel for other people. Books give us a safe place to deal with messy feelings like grief, loneliness, and doubt. When a society takes that away to keep everything "calm," people don't end up happy—they end up hollow, isolated, and completely numb.
* **How to connect it to *Fahrenheit 451* (Synthesis):**
  * Look at **Mildred Montag**. The very first time we see her in Part 1, she is doing the exact same thing Collins describes. She is lying on her bed in the pitch-dark room, curled up with her little "Seashell" earbuds blasting white noise and radio chatter, having just swallowed thirty sleeping pills. 
  * Mildred is literally dying of sadness inside, but she can't talk about it or even admit it the next morning because her society has burned away every book that could help her understand what depression is. 
  * Compare that with **Clarisse**, who actually walks outside in the rain, tilts her face up, and tastes the raindrops. Books and nature connect people to real feelings; censorship forces people to curl into cold corners.

### Source 2: "The Pedestrian" by Ray Bradbury (Short Story)
* **What page of packet:** Page 2
* **The Quote to use:**
  > *"...tomb-like houses at night... where the people sat like the dead, the gray or multicolored lights touching their faces, but never really touching them."* (Bradbury p. 2)
* **What it really means (Inference):**
  * Leonard Mead is the only person walking outside on a chilly November night. Every single house he passes is dark, except for the flickering glow of a TV screen.
  * Bradbury literally calls the houses *"tombs"* and the people *"the dead."* Just having a heartbeat doesn't mean you're really alive. These people are zombies in their own living rooms.
  * The light touches their skin, but it *"never really touches them."* That means screen entertainment looks bright and colorful, but it never reaches their hearts or makes them think.
* **Why this proves censorship is dangerous (Analysis):**
  * True censorship doesn't always need armed guards on every street corner. The most dangerous censorship happens when society feeds people endless, mindless entertainment so they stop thinking for themselves. 
  * When everyone is glued to a screen alone at home, neighborhoods fall silent, public discussion dies, and nobody talks to their neighbors. And once people stop talking to each other, an oppressive government has zero opposition.
* **How to connect it to *Fahrenheit 451* (Synthesis):**
  * Leonard Mead is basically the first draft of **Clarisse McClellan** and **Guy Montag**. Just like Mead gets arrested by a robotic police car just for walking outside, Clarisse is treated like a weird criminal because she likes to walk, smell autumn leaves, and talk with her family on the porch. 
  * Those *"tomb-like houses"* in "The Pedestrian" turn into the parlor rooms in *Fahrenheit 451*, where Mildred sits surrounded by three full walls of screaming soap operas, calling actors on TV her "family" even though she doesn't care if her real husband lives or dies.

### Source 3: "Dover Beach" by Matthew Arnold (Poem)
* **What page of packet:** Page 3
* **The Quote to use:**
  > *"Swept with confused alarms of struggle and flight,*  
  > *Where ignorant armies clash by night."* (Arnold lines 35–37)
* **What it really means (Inference):**
  * Arnold is looking out over the English Channel, listening to the waves pull pebbles back and forth. He realizes that faith, shared meaning, and moral truth are pulling back like the tide, leaving the world exposed and cold.
  * He compares the world to a dark battlefield where *"ignorant armies clash by night."* When soldiers fight in total darkness, they can't see who is on their side and who is the enemy. They just blindly hack away at each other. Ignorance doesn't create peace—it creates senseless violence.
* **Why this proves censorship is dangerous (Analysis):**
  * When a culture bans literature, history, and philosophy, it throws away its moral compass. Without books that tell honest stories about right and wrong, people lose their sense of direction. They have no idea how to spot evil or question a terrible war. They just follow the crowd in the dark.
* **How to connect it to *Fahrenheit 451* (Synthesis):**
  * This is the **exact poem Montag reads to Mildred and her friends (Mrs. Phelps and Mrs. Bowles) in Part 2**. 
  * Before Montag reads it, the women are sitting in the parlor laughing about their husbands going off to war like it's a game, and talking about how having children is like throwing laundry in a washer. But the second Montag finishes reading "Dover Beach," **Mrs. Phelps bursts into tears**. 
  * Why does she cry? Because Arnold’s words hit a nerve that the TV walls never could. The poem shatters her fake happiness and forces her to feel the deep terror of her husband fighting in a war he might not survive. 
  * And at the end of Part 3, Arnold's "ignorant armies" come true: enemy jets bomb Montag's city into dust in a matter of seconds, wiping out millions of people who were too busy watching TV to realize war was right above them.

### Source 4: The Myth of Daedalus & Icarus (Greek Myth)
* **What page of packet:** Page 4
* **The Quote to use:**
  > *Beatty in Part 3:* *"Old Montag wanted to fly near the sun and now that he’s burnt his damn wings, he wonders why."* (Bradbury p. 107)
* **What it really means (Inference):**
  * In the myth, Daedalus builds wings of feathers and wax for his son Icarus and warns him: don't fly too close to the sun, or the wax will melt. Icarus gets arrogant, flies too high, his wings fall apart, and he drowns in the ocean.
  * In *Fahrenheit 451*, Captain Beatty brings the fire truck to Montag's own house and mocks him with this myth. Beatty is trying to make Montag feel like a foolish child. He wants Montag to believe that reading books was just selfish arrogance, and that anyone who questions society is doomed to fail.
* **Why this proves censorship is dangerous (Analysis):**
  * Notice the hypocrisy: Beatty is the chief book burner, yet he has read all the great classics! Censors love to keep knowledge for themselves so they can manipulate everyone else. 
  * By calling curiosity "flying too close to the sun," authorities convince normal people that asking questions is dangerous and that staying ignorant is the only safe way to live.
* **How to connect it to *Fahrenheit 451* (Synthesis):**
  * Bradbury flips the myth on its head. In the myth, Icarus drowns and dies. But Montag doesn't drown. 
  * Yes, Montag's "wings" burn—his house is torched, his job is gone, and Mildred leaves him. But burning down that fake life is what saves him! 
  * When Montag jumps into the river to escape the Mechanical Hound, the water doesn't drown him like Icarus. The river washes off the smell of kerosene, hides him from the police, and carries him into the countryside where he meets Granger and the scholars. Montag ends up memorizing the *Book of Ecclesiastes*. He starts out as a fireman burning books, and ends up becoming a living book himself.

## The 3 Best Quotes from Fahrenheit 451 (For Packet Page 5)

Put these in your packet on Page 5 to back up your essay:

1. **Captain Beatty explaining how censorship started (Part 1, p. 55):**
   > *"There didn't come from the government down. There was no dictum, no declaration, no censorship, to start with, no! Technology, mass exploitation, and minority pressure carried the trick, thank God. Today, thanks to them, you can stay happy all the time."*
   * *Why this is gold for your essay:* It proves that censorship didn't start with a dictator holding a gun. It started because regular people wanted life to be easy, fast, and fun. People gave up books voluntarily so nobody's feelings would get hurt. That makes censorship terrifying: society did it to itself.

2. **Guy Montag telling Mildred why they need to wake up (Part 1, p. 49):**
   > *"We need to be really bothered once in a while. How long is it since you were really bothered? About something important, about something real?"*
   * *Why this is gold for your essay:* This is the heart of Bradbury's whole book. If you never let yourself feel uncomfortable or sad, you can never care about other people. Books bother us, and that's exactly why we need them.

3. **Granger explaining the Phoenix (Part 3, p. 156):**
   > *"There was a silly damn bird called a Phoenix... every few hundred years he built a pyre and burnt himself up... But every time he burnt himself up he sprang out of the ashes, he got himself born all over again. And it looks like we're doing the same thing... But we've got one damn thing the Phoenix never had. We know the damn silly thing we just did."*
   * *Why this is gold for your essay:* Granger points out that humanity keeps burning its own cities down in wars. The only reason we don't have to keep doing that is because books record our mistakes. When you censor books, you erase the memory of what went wrong, and history just repeats itself.

## 5-Paragraph Essay Blueprint for Wednesday

Write this with confidence during your period. Keep it organized and clear.

### Paragraph 1: Introduction (5–7 minutes)
* **Hook:** Most people think censorship is enforced by cruel governments, but the truth is scarier: societies often choose censorship themselves because they'd rather be entertained than face uncomfortable truths.
* **Introduce the texts:** In *Fahrenheit 451*, Ray Bradbury shows a future where books are burned to keep everyone happy and docile. Paired with companion works like Billy Collins's poem *"Rain,"* Bradbury's own short story *"The Pedestrian,"* and Matthew Arnold's *"Dover Beach,"* the danger of censorship becomes crystal clear.
* **Your Thesis Statement (Write this clearly!):**
  > *Although advocates of censorship claim that banning controversial material creates harmony and keeps citizens happy, censorship is actually dangerous to society because it leaves people emotionally hollow and numb, isolates individuals inside mindless entertainment, and erases the historical memory needed to stop civilization from destroying itself.*

---

### Paragraph 2: Body 1 — Emotional Numbness & Disconnection (10 minutes)
* **Main Point:** Censorship doesn't make people peaceful; it strips away their ability to handle real emotions.
* **Evidence from "Rain":** Billy Collins writes that when books disappear, people are left *"curling up... quietly in corners and doorways."* Notice how Collins turns the relaxing idea of curling up with a book into an image of terrified, helpless people with no voice left to speak.
* **Connection to F451:** That is Mildred Montag to a tee. She curls up in bed every night with her Seashell earbuds, drowning out silence until she overdoses on thirty sleeping pills. She is miserable, but because her world burned all the books, she doesn't even have the vocabulary to understand her own sadness. 
* **Wrap-up sentence:** When a society outlaws the messy, uncomfortable truths found in literature, citizens don't become happy—they turn into walking ghosts who hurt inside and don't know why.

---

### Paragraph 3: Body 2 — Solitary Confinement Inside Our Own Living Rooms (10 minutes)
* **Main Point:** Censorship breaks apart communities and turns lively neighborhoods into digital graveyards.
* **Evidence from "The Pedestrian":** In "The Pedestrian," Leonard Mead walks down silent streets where houses are like *"tombs, ill-lit by television light"* and the people sit *"like the dead."* The colored light touches their faces, but *"never really touches them."* 
* **Connection to F451:** Those dark houses are identical to the parlor rooms in *Fahrenheit 451*. Mildred spends all day talking to actors on screens she calls her "family," but she doesn't even know her real husband. Meanwhile, Clarisse McClellan is branded as weird and antisocial simply because she likes walking outside, looking at the moon, and having real conversations. 
* **Wrap-up sentence:** Censorship replaces real human connection with loud, shallow screens, making sure people stay too distracted to ever organize or push back.

---

### Paragraph 4: Body 3 — Erasing History Guarantees Destruction (10 minutes)
* **Main Point:** Without books to remind us of past mistakes, society loses its moral compass and marches straight into war.
* **Evidence from "Dover Beach":** Matthew Arnold warns that when spiritual truth and literature fade away, humanity is left on a dark battlefield *"where ignorant armies clash by night."* When Montag reads this poem out loud, Mrs. Phelps starts crying hysterically because the poem shatters her fake bubble and forces her to feel the terror of her husband fighting in the war.
* **Connection to F451:** That ignorant battlefield comes to life at the end of the novel when jet bombers wipe out the city in a split second. As Granger explains, humans are like the Phoenix that burns itself up every few hundred years. The only thing that can save us from repeating that cycle is keeping books around so we remember our mistakes.
* **Wrap-up sentence:** Censorship doesn't protect the peace; it blinds people to danger until the bombs are already falling.

---

### Paragraph 5: Conclusion (5 minutes)
* **Restate your thesis in fresh words:** At the end of the day, burning books doesn't create a peaceful utopia. It creates people who can't feel, neighborhoods that look like cemeteries, and a society that repeats its worst disasters.
* **Tie your sources together:** From Collins's curled-up people and Mead's silent streets, to Mrs. Phelps weeping over Arnold's poetry and Montag rising from the city's ashes, literature proves that being "bothered" is what keeps us human.
* **Final Thought:** True freedom doesn't come from running away from hard ideas. It comes from having the courage to read them, talk about them, and learn from them.
